# $15K in two months — selling the inventory, not the deals

Written 22 Oct 2026, at Sid's reframe: *"I have insane inventory — 10 newsletters and 50
LinkedIn posts through the end of the year. Each one can earn $300+. How do we fill it?"*

The reframe is right, and it changes the whole motion.

| | Old model | Inventory model |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | 1 deal @ $1,500 | 1 slot @ $250–$300 |
| Deals needed for $15K | **10 closes** | **~50 slot sales** |
| Sales cycle each | 2–3 weeks, named buyer, negotiation | minutes, self-serve, no negotiation |
| Record so far | 1 close in 46 days | untested |

The old model has produced one deal in 46 days. It is not going to produce ten more in eight
weeks. So the question is the right one.

---

## 🔴 First, the thing that must be settled: $300 is not a discount, it's a different SKU

`CLAUDE.md` rule 2 says never discount. Selling a **dedicated LinkedIn post** for $300 when
Airtable and Optimizely each paid **$1,500** would not be a discount — it would be the
permanent end of the $1,500 rate, because sold prices leak.

**The fix is to sell smaller units, not cheaper versions of the same unit.** Industry rate
cards already do exactly this, at roughly these multipliers of the primary slot:

| Placement | Multiplier | On a $750 newsletter primary |
|---|---:|---:|
| Primary / dedicated | 1.0× | **$750** |
| Secondary | 0.5–0.6× | **$375–450** |
| Classified | 0.25–0.30× | **$190–225** |

So the honest rate card gains three new lines and loses nothing:

```
NEW — small units
Newsletter classified (3 per issue, 40 words + link):        $250
LinkedIn in-post mention (one line inside a normal post):     $300
Newsletter secondary slot (mid-body, 80 words):               $400

UNCHANGED — the premium units
Newsletter primary / dedicated slot:                          $750
Dedicated LinkedIn post:                                    $1,500
```

**A $300 mention inside a post Sid was writing anyway is a genuinely different product from a
$1,500 post built around a brand.** That distinction is what protects the rate card. It needs
to be stated that way in every pitch, or the $1,500 line dies.

⚠️ **Sid's decision, not the desk's.** This is a rate-card change by the principal, not a
concession to a buyer. The desk cannot make it. Confirm and it goes live.

---

## The ceiling nobody has priced in

50 LinkedIn posts × $300 = $15,000 **on paper only.** Filling all 50 would mean every single
post for the rest of the year carries an ad. That destroys the thing being sold.

Published guidance on ad density lands at **2–3 placements per send maximum** before
engagement degrades. Applying something similar to LinkedIn:

| Channel | Inventory | Sellable without damage | At rate | Realistic ceiling |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Newsletter classifieds | 10 issues × 3 | 30 slots | $250 | $7,500 |
| Newsletter primary | 10 issues × 1 | 10 slots | $750 | $7,500 |
| LinkedIn mentions | 50 posts | **~15 (30% of posts)** | $300 | $4,500 |
| LinkedIn dedicated | 50 posts | **~4** | $1,500 | $6,000 |
| | | | **Total capacity** | **$25,500** |

**$15K needs roughly a 60% fill rate on that capacity, from a standing start, in eight
weeks.** That is the real target. It is possible, and it is not easy.

## 💡 The demand problem, and the one channel that works while sourcing is blocked

Capacity is not the constraint. **Demand is.** And Apify is hard-blocked until 4 November, so
cold sourcing cannot create that demand.

**But Sid's own audience contains the buyers.** 20,100 newsletter subscribers and 77,565
LinkedIn followers, in AI and product, full of founders, growth leads and marketers who hold
budget. Newsletter operators fill inventory this way routinely and it costs nothing to try.

### Do this first, this week

1. **One LinkedIn post announcing the inventory.** Not a media kit — a plain post: here is the
   audience, here are the slots left in 2026, here are the prices, here is the link. Sid's
   77K reach is the cheapest advertising channel he will ever have for his own ad inventory.
2. **A standing P.S. in every newsletter issue.** "Three classified slots left in November —
   $250 each, reply or book here." Ten issues of compounding ask, zero marginal effort.
3. **A self-serve checkout so buying takes sixty seconds.** This matters more than it sounds:
   **every deal lost in this pipeline died because a human had to reply.** A Stripe payment
   link plus a date picker removes the desk and Sid from the critical path entirely.

⚠️ **Self-serve does not create demand** — the vendors who sell it say so plainly. It converts
demand that steps 1 and 2 create. Both halves are needed.

Tooling, if useful: Sponsonizer (booking page, 7% per booking or $99/yr for 0%) or SponsorCal.
A Stripe payment link and a Calendly would also do it for nothing.

## 💡 Prepay bundles — because cash in November beats an invoice in January

The 30 November first-touch deadline exists because invoices take weeks. **Prepaid inventory
has no such problem: the money arrives at purchase.**

```
Starter   — 3 newsletter classifieds                          $600   (save $150)
Q4 Mixed  — 4 classifieds + 2 LinkedIn mentions             $1,500   (save $400)
Takeover  — 1 newsletter primary + 1 dedicated LinkedIn     $2,000   (save $250)
Cohort    — official tool of the Accelerator + 1 primary    $2,500
```

Bundle discounts are not rate-card discounts — they are paid for with **volume and cash up
front**, which is the normal trade. Seven Q4 Mixed bundles is $10,500.

## Backfill, for the slots that stay empty

Unsold inventory is worth more than nothing. Both BuySellAds' Newsletter Network and Admailr
pitch automatic filling of unsold dates with matched demand at discounted rates, with a **floor
price** the publisher sets.

The Beehiiv ad network is already doing a version of this and the numbers are small — offers
have run **$1.50–$3.75 per click** and roughly **$6–$23 estimated per send**. That is not
revenue; it is scrap value. But it is free money on a slot that was going empty, and **seven
rotations in thirteen days have been flagged and none claimed.** Ten issues × ~$15 = ~$150.
Worth claiming on sight; not worth planning around.

## The honest risks

1. **Fill rate is unproven.** Every number above assumes 50–60% of capacity sells. Zero slots
   have ever been sold at these prices. The first week of the announcement post and the P.S.
   will tell us the real rate, and the plan should be re-cut on that evidence rather than on
   this table.
2. **The $1,500 rate is the thing at risk.** If a $300 mention is ever described as "a
   LinkedIn post," the premium rate is gone. The SKU language has to hold in every pitch.
3. **Audience cost is real.** 15 sponsored mentions across 50 posts and 30 classifieds across
   10 issues is a visible increase in commercial content. It is within published density
   norms, but it is not free.
4. **Nothing here fixes sending.** Self-serve checkout routes around it, which is the point —
   but the cohort pitches, the primaries and the Tiiny Host campaign all still require emails
   to actually leave the mailbox.

## The three things only Sid can do

1. **Approve the new small-unit rate card** ($250 classified / $300 mention / $400 secondary,
   with $750 and $1,500 untouched). The desk cannot change prices.
2. **Post the inventory announcement** to LinkedIn, and add the P.S. to the newsletter.
3. **Decide whether the cohort is sponsorable at all** — it is his course and his students,
   and selling access to them is a judgement call about the product, not a revenue question.
