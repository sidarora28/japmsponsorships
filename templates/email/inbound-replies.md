# Inbound reply templates

Placeholders in `{{braces}}`. Verified numbers only — **25.4% open, never 35%.**
Every one of these is a *draft* for Sid to review.

---

## 1. Inquiry with no number → quote card

> **Subject:** Re: {{their subject}}
>
> Hi {{name}},
>
> Thanks for reaching out — {{brand}} is a good fit for this audience.
>
> Rates are $1,500 for a LinkedIn post, $1,000 for an X thread, and $750 for a newsletter slot.
> The LinkedIn placement is the strongest of the three.
>
> For context: ~75,000 LinkedIn followers and 20,170 newsletter subscribers at a 25.4% open
> rate, roughly 60% US/UK. The audience is AI product managers and product builders — people
> who buy software, which is why dev and AI tools do well here.
>
> I write JustAnotherPM alongside my day job as Head of Product for Gen AI at Yelp, so these
> read as a practitioner recommendation rather than a creator ad. That tends to be the part
> that works.
>
> Happy to look at dates if the rate works.
>
> Sid

**The rate is in the third line on purpose.** Airtable closed in under 24 hours because the
number came early and plainly.

---

## 2. Below-card offer → counter at card

> Hi {{name}},
>
> Thanks for the offer. The rate for a LinkedIn placement is $1,500 — I keep it consistent
> across partners, so I'd want to hold there rather than work backwards from a budget.
>
> What you're getting at that number: ~75,000 LinkedIn followers, roughly 60% US/UK, and an
> audience of AI PMs and product builders who expense software. It's a narrow, senior list
> rather than a broad one.
>
> If $1,500 works, I can look at {{month}} dates.
>
> Sid

**One counter, at card. Never split the difference.** If they hold below, it goes to Sid — the
desk cannot accept below card.

---

## 3. Lowball (well under card — e.g. Coworker.ai's $480)

Same as #2, but do not treat the number as an anchor to move from. State card, give the
reason, stop. If they come back still low → **→ SID**.

---

## 4. Reviving a stale thread (Promoted, Recall, Rezonant)

> Hi {{name}},
>
> Apologies for the slow reply on this — it got away from me.
>
> If {{brand}} is still looking at a placement, I'd love to pick it up. LinkedIn is $1,500 and
> I have {{month}} availability.
>
> Completely understand if the moment has passed — just let me know either way.
>
> Sid

**Acknowledge the delay in one line and move on.** They can see the timestamps; pretending is
worse than owning it. No grovelling — one sentence, then the offer.

---

## 5. Accepting at or above card

> Hi {{name}},
>
> That works — happy to move ahead at {{amount}}.
>
> To confirm: {{placement}}, going live {{proposed date}}. Send over the brief and any links
> or assets and I'll get the concept to you by {{date}}.
>
> Sid

⚠️ **Dates are Sid's to confirm.** Propose in the draft; he commits.
If the offer is *above* card, take their number. Never negotiate against him.

---

## 6. Bundle or discount request → do not draft

**→ SID.** No counter, no "let me check." The desk has no authority here. Hand it over with
the thread and what they asked for.

The 3-post packages are also currently priced *above* three singles (see
`knowledge/rate-card.md`), so quote singles until that's resolved.

---

## Voice notes

- Short. A media buyer decides in about fifteen seconds.
- Rate early, stated as a fact. No "we could probably do something around."
- Justify with the audience, never with enthusiasm.
- The Yelp practitioner line is the differentiator — it's what a competing creator can't copy.
- Never invent a metric, case study, or past result.
