## 1. The LinkedIn post — announcing the inventory

Written as a teaching post, not an advert, for one reason: a post that says *"my slots are
open"* earns nothing and gets no reach. A post that teaches vendors **how to buy a creator
sponsorship properly** gets saved by the exact people who buy them, and the inventory notice
rides along at the end.

It also does something a pure advert cannot. Every number in it is the honest one — median not
mean, trailing-three-months not YTD, the real geography split. The discipline this repo has
been enforcing internally becomes the sales argument.

**Measured: 1,904 characters · Flesch-Kincaid grade 4.6 · 0 em dashes · 0 rhetorical questions
· 5 items matching the "5 numbers" in the hook.**

```
Most creators will not show you these 5 numbers before you take their rate card. Here is how to ask for each one.

If you buy newsletter or LinkedIn sponsorships, this is your checklist.

■ The median post, not the average
↳ Ask for the middle number across the last 15 posts. Averages get pulled up by one post that went wide, so a seller quoting 5,000 may have a typical post nearer 3,000. Ask for the full list and find the middle yourself.

■ The open rate from the last three months
↳ Newsletter open rates drift down as a list ages, so a yearly figure flatters the seller. Ask for the trailing three months. That is the number your campaign gets measured against when the report lands.

■ Where the readers live
↳ Ask for the country split before you sign. A list that is 45% US and 15% UK is a different product from one that is 70% India, and neither of those is wrong. It only becomes a problem when you learn it from the report.

■ What the last sponsor in your category got
↳ Ask for the impressions, clicks and saves from one real past placement. Any seller who has run one has the screenshot. A seller who will not show you one has either never run it or did not like the result.

■ Who writes the post
↳ Ask whether the writer still does the job their readers do. An endorsement from someone who ships product every week reads differently from one written by a full time creator, and your buyers can tell in two lines.

Asking all five takes one email. Most sellers go quiet on the first one.

Mine, since it is only fair. Newsletter: about 20,100 subscribers, 25.8% open rate over the last three months. LinkedIn: 77,565 followers, median post 3,298 impressions. Readers are 45% US and 15% UK. I am Head of Product for Gen AI at Yelp.

Newsletter slots and LinkedIn mentions are open for the rest of 2026. My email is in the first comment.

Save this before your next creator sponsorship.
```

**First comment** (links go here, never in the body):

```

```

## 2. The standing newsletter P.S.

Goes at the foot of **every issue** from the next send until the end of the year. Ten issues
left; this is ten free impressions in front of a self-selected list of AI people, at zero cost
and zero editorial weight.

```
P.S. I keep a small number of sponsor slots in this newsletter and on LinkedIn, and a few
are still open through December. If you sell something AI product managers use, reply to
this email and I will send the rates and the dates.
```

Keep it to three lines and do not dress it up. The job is to be present, not persuasive.

## 3. What to do when someone replies

The reply is the whole point, and it is where the last six weeks went wrong: inbound arrived
and expired. **Rule: a rate reply goes out the same day.**

Template, using only the published rates:

```
Hi [name],

Thanks for asking. Here is what is open and what it costs.

Dedicated LinkedIn post, built around you: $1,500
Newsletter primary slot, 10 issues left this year: $750

Audience, verified and I will send screenshots on request:
- Newsletter: ~20,100 subscribers, 25.8% open rate trailing three months
- LinkedIn: 77,565 followers, median post 3,298 impressions
- 45% US, 30% India, 15% UK

I write everything myself and I am Head of Product for Gen AI at Yelp, so the post comes
from someone who ships AI product rather than someone who writes about it.

Which slot and which month? Dates go in order of payment.

Sid
```

🔴 **Do not add the small-unit prices to this template until Sid has approved them.** The
proposed $250 classified / $300 mention / $400 secondary lines are in
`knowledge/inventory-plan-15k.md` awaiting his decision. Quoting them before that is the desk
setting a rate card, which it has no authority to do, and the $1,500 line is what is at risk
if a smaller number goes out loose.
